### Cloud Computing Overview

Cloud computing delivers on-demand IT resources over the internet with pay-as-you-go pricing. AWS provides three primary service models — IaaS, PaaS, and SaaS — alongside global infrastructure for low-latency delivery. Key benefits include elasticity, economies of scale, and reduced capital expenditure.

### How It Works
- Providers host shared physical infrastructure that is virtualized and provisioned on demand.
- Customers request resources through APIs and consoles, scaling up or down as needed.
- Billing is usage-based, converting capital expenditure into operational expenditure.
- Global data centers place resources close to users for lower latency.

### Key Characteristics
- **On-Demand Self-Service**: Provision resources without human interaction.
- **Broad Network Access**: Reach services over standard internet protocols.
- **Resource Pooling**: Multi-tenant infrastructure shared among customers.
- **Rapid Elasticity**: Scale capacity up or down quickly.
- **Measured Service**: Usage is metered and billed accordingly.

### Service Models
- [[IaaS]]: Virtualized compute, storage, and networking (e.g., [[EC2]]).
- [[PaaS]]: Managed platforms for application development (e.g., [[Elastic Beanstalk]]).
- [[SaaS]]: Ready-to-use software delivered over the internet (e.g., WorkSpaces).

### Common Use Cases
- Migrating on-premises data centers to reduce cost and increase agility.
- Building and scaling applications without capacity planning.
- Running global, low-latency services on distributed infrastructure.
- Innovating quickly with managed services and machine learning.

### Pricing & Limits
- Pay-as-you-go pricing with no upfront commitment (unless discounts are chosen).
- Economies of scale let providers offer lower unit costs than on-premises.
- Management responsibility decreases from IaaS to PaaS to SaaS.
